- **Across all years, a whole franchise does worse than a standalone film.** From 2000 onward, which is today's market and the one I relied on, a franchise film does better one by one, but a whole franchise only ties a standalone. In both periods a gap of zero is plausible. Franchises are not the safer kind of purchase.
- **Ten of the 23 franchises on your list are missing films**, usually weaker later sequels. Valued whole, Star Wars falls from 10.8 to 6.2. Every figure here uses the whole franchise.
- **No director or lead actor reliably signals a hit since 2000** once the franchise is set aside: Christopher Nolan's films beat the typical film 29% of the time, Peter Jackson's 50%. Do not pay extra for a name.

## What I recommend

**Buy three specific franchises, not franchises as a category, and keep about 30% of the budget for standalone films.** Each ceiling is that title's share of the $14M if you bought all eight, set by its box-office record against a typical recent standalone film.

| Title | Why it made the cut | Walk away above |
|---|---|---|
| *The Lord of the Rings* (3 films) | Best franchise on every measure; weakest film 9.4 | $3.3M |
| *The Avengers* (4 films) | 7.1 overall; weakest film 6.0; rated 8.2 | $2.8M |
| *Harry Potter* (8 films) | Largest library; every film above 3.7 | $3.6M |
| *Inception* | 5.2; $839M earned; rated 8.4 | $0.6M |
| *Interstellar* | 4.5; $747M earned; rated 8.5 | $0.5M |
| *Spirited Away* | 14.5 on a $19M budget; rated 8.5 | $1.7M |
| *The Incredibles* | 6.9; has a sequel, so may only be sold as a pair | $0.8M |
| *Monsters, Inc.* | 5.0; has a sequel; its figures look rounded | $0.6M |

**Deal note, The Lord of the Rings.** The three films cost $266M and earned $2.92 billion. Pay up to about twice the per-film price of a typical recent standalone: about six standalone films' worth, roughly a quarter of the budget ($3.3M if you buy the whole shortlist). The number that drove this is **9.4**, what the weakest film, *The Fellowship of the Ring*, earned per dollar, against 4.5 for a typical standalone. You pay for every film in a bundle, so the weakest sets the price.

## What I could not stand behind

- **Licence prices.** I have box office, not what the rights cost. The ceilings tell you when a quote is out of line with the rest of the list, not whether it is cheap.
- **Availability.** I don't know whether these rights are for sale, or whether studios will sell a franchise without its newest films.
- **A list of hits only.** Every film earned more than it cost, so all figures are best cases; only the comparisons are fair.
- **Old money and small numbers.** Figures are not adjusted for inflation, and since 2000 there are only 12 standalone films and 11 whole franchises. One film can move a result.
- **The list itself.** 127 lines became 112 films: 7 repeats, 3 blank or invalid, 5 unknown to the database. No real film was lost.

Data as of 17–18 November 2026; a live re-check of 12 films found no differences.
