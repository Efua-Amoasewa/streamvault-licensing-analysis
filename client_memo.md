# Memo to Kwame

**From:** Esther Acquandoh
**Date:** 18 November 2026
**Subject:** Franchise bundles or standalone hits for the $14M licensing budget

## Recommendation

**Buy three specific franchises, not franchises as a category, and keep about 30% of the budget for standalone films.** Since 2000, franchise films have done somewhat better than standalone films one by one, but a typical whole franchise, as a studio sells it, only matches a typical standalone. So the case rests on three bundles whose *weakest* film beats a typical standalone, not on bundles being safer.

## The comparison, both ways. The answer changes with the window

| Box office earned per $1 of production budget | All years | 2000 onward |
|---|---|---|
| Typical franchise film | 5.5 | 5.1 |
| Typical standalone film | 5.0 | 4.5 |
| **Typical whole franchise, as sold** | **4.4** | **4.5** |
| Whole franchise ahead when I re-ran it on 10,000 reshuffled lists | 4 in 10 times | 6 or 7 in 10 times |

**Across all years, a whole franchise does worse than a standalone film.** Standalone films also win on average (10.5 against 7.9), because of old hits like Jaws (1975) and The Good, the Bad and the Ugly (1966). **From 2000 onward, the typical franchise film leads (5.1 against 4.5), but the typical whole franchise only ties a standalone (4.5 each)**, and either way a gap of zero is plausible. I relied on 2000 onward because it is today's market. Ten of the 23 franchises on the list are missing released films, usually weaker later sequels; valued whole, as a studio sells them, Star Wars falls from 10.8 to 6.2 and Jurassic Park from 8.9 to 5.0. My figures use the whole franchise.

## Shortlist and walk-away ceilings

The ceilings are each title's share of the $14M if you bought all eight, set by its box-office record against a typical recent standalone film. They are relative limits, not market prices.

| Title | Why it made the cut | Ceiling |
|---|---|---|
| *The Lord of the Rings* (3 films) | Best franchise on every measure; weakest film 9.4 | $3.3M |
| *The Avengers* (4 films) | 7.1 overall; weakest film 6.0; rated 8.2 | $2.8M |
| *Harry Potter* (8 films) | Largest library; every film above 3.7 | $3.6M (less per film than a typical standalone) |
| *Inception* | 5.2; $839M earned; rated 8.4 | $0.6M |
| *Interstellar* | 4.5; $747M earned; rated 8.5 | $0.5M |
| *Spirited Away* | 14.5 on a $19M budget; rated 8.5 | $1.7M |
| *The Incredibles* | 6.9; has a sequel, so may only be sold as a pair | $0.8M |
| *Monsters, Inc.* | 5.0; has a sequel; its figures look rounded | $0.6M |

That splits the budget roughly $9.7M on franchises and $4.3M on standalones.

## Deal note: *The Lord of the Rings*

The three films cost $266M and earned $2.92 billion. Pay up to about **twice the per-film price of a typical recent standalone**: about six standalone films' worth, roughly a quarter of the budget ($3.3M if you buy the whole shortlist). Walk away above that. The number that drove this is **9.4**, what the *weakest* film, *The Fellowship of the Ring*, earned per dollar of budget, against 4.5 for a typical standalone. In a bundle you pay for every film, so the weakest sets the price.

## Directors and lead actors

Do not pay extra for a name. Since 2000, no director or lead actor reliably signals a hit once the franchise is set aside: Christopher Nolan's films beat the typical film 29% of the time, Peter Jackson's 50%. Across all years Steven Spielberg, Robert Zemeckis and Tom Hanks pass, but on 1975–1997 films or, for Hanks, mostly Toy Story.

## What I could not stand behind

- **Licence prices.** I have box office, not what the rights cost. The ceilings tell you when a quote is out of line with the rest of the list, not whether it is cheap.
- **Availability.** I don't know whether these rights are for sale, or whether studios will sell a franchise without its newest films.
- **A list of hits only.** Every film earned more than it cost, so all figures are best cases. Only the comparisons between options are fair.
- **Old money.** Figures are not adjusted for inflation, and box office is not streaming viewing.
- **Small numbers.** Only 12 standalone films and 11 franchises of three or more films made entirely since 2000. One film can move a result.
- **The candidate list.** 127 lines became 112 films: 7 repeats, 3 blank or invalid, 5 unknown to the database. No real film was lost.

The data is as of 17–18 November 2026. A live re-check of 12 films found no differences from our stored copy.
