# ADR-005: Call a director or lead actor a reliable signal only with 3+ films across 2+ series and a 75% hit rate

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Date:** 2026-11-17
- **Decision owner:** Esther Acquandoh

## Context

Kwame asked whether talent is a reason to buy. A person's films share a franchise, so a good run may just be the franchise (Elijah Wood's three films are all The Lord of the Rings).

## Decision

For each person, count the share of their films whose return beats the window's middle film (the hit rate). Call a person reliable only if they have at least 3 films, in at least 2 separate series, with a hit rate of 75% or more.

## Alternatives considered

### Alternative A: Hit rate alone

- **How it would work:** Rank people by the share of their films that beat the typical film.
- **Why it is attractive:** Simple.
- **Why rejected:** It would crown Elijah Wood (3 of 3) and Ray Romano (3 of 3), who each appear in only one series. That measures the franchise, not the person.

### Alternative B: A lower bar (two in three films)

- **How it would work:** Hit rate of 67% instead of 75%.
- **Why it is attractive:** More names for negotiators.
- **Why rejected:** From 2000 onward it adds only Robert Downey Jr. (5 of 7 across 2 series, 71%), whose films are almost all one connected Marvel universe. One film either way changes his verdict.

## Consequences

- **What this buys:** A test that separates the person from the franchise.
- **What this costs:** Very few people can pass it in 112 films.
- **What it costs the client if I am wrong:** A genuinely bankable person is overlooked.
- **What would make me revisit this:** A larger list where more people have films across several series.

## What this survives

The answer depends on the window, and the memo says so.

- **All years:** three people pass: Steven Spielberg (8 films, 4 series, 88%), Robert Zemeckis (4 films, 2 series, 100%) and Tom Hanks (5 films, 2 series, 80%). Spielberg's and Zemeckis's films are all but one from 1975–1997, the era whose returns are inflated (F-03). Tom Hanks passes on four Toy Story films (as a voice) plus Forrest Gump, so his record is mostly one franchise. Most of these films sit inside bundles already priced on their own merits (Indiana Jones, Jurassic Park, Back to the Future, Toy Story).
- **2000 onward:** nobody passes. Christopher Nolan has 7 films across 5 series and only 29% beat the typical film; Peter Jackson has 6 films in 2 series at 50%.

So talent is not a reason to pay more for a recent title. For negotiators the useful lever is the reverse: a studio asking a premium for a name has no support for it in this list. At about 500 titles more people will qualify and the same rule applies.
